# Art and model maintenance

The authoritative editable pixel source is `tools/author_assets.py`, and the editable geometry is `art/lumberton-model.json` plus `art/lumberton.bbmodel`. Runtime geometry and animation are in `client/LumbertonModel.java`. Regenerate the resource artwork with `python tools/author_assets.py`.

- Final standing height: **24 model units = 1.5 blocks**.
- One model unit and one UV unit: **1/16 block and one source pixel**.
- Collision box: **0.7 × 1.5 blocks**. Eye height: **1.29 blocks**.
- Atlas: **64 × 64** per expression. The unused space provides layout room, not increased pixel density.
- Block materials and item sprites: **16 × 16**. Partial block faces use proportionate UV rectangles.
- Geometry uses integer cuboid dimensions. No whole-model shrinking, blurred image resampling, or smooth material gradients are used.

| Part | Dimensions in model units | UV origin |

| Torso | 8 × 9 × 6 | 0, 14 |
| Each arm | 3 × 13 × 4 | 32, 0 / 48, 0 |

The head pivots at its base. The arm rig anchors the actual tool stack; the axe is not part of the geometry. Idle sway, opposing walking legs/arms, head tracking, chopping swings, and returning/unloading poses use the same rig.

The face occupies a 6 × 6 front UV rectangle starting at (6, 6). Expression atlases are `idle`, `smile`, `brow`, `focused`, `frown`, and `anger`. Calm at home reuses neutral. Full matching atlases avoid overlay z-fighting. Server state selects the expression; smiles last 50 ticks and hurt reactions 35 ticks. Persistent alerts take priority over smiles.

The torso consists of ordinary horizontal plank rows, with a small amber marking. The supplied reference's brick motif was deliberately excluded. Six enlarged atlas previews in `art` are inspection aids; the game loads only the original 64 × 64 files.

The totem uses adjoining cuboids to approximate an octagonal column. Wood sections stop at the iron bands to avoid coplanar faces. Its top glows. The protection curtain uses a smooth opacity fade in rendering; this is separate from the pixel-art material textures.

`F:\Minecraft\Automatons` contains organized copies of references, model sources, textures, documentation, and actual in-game captures.


## Redesign assets

The new empty/full cores, head, torso, arm, leg, upgrade plate and complete Automaton item are native 16×16 pixel sprites generated by the same editable source. Infuser black/white materials are also 16×16. Its body and four claws reach eight model units (half a block). The floating core is a rendered item above the claws; progress controls glow and transfer pulses.

The Automaton Pedestal renders the complete Automaton item continuously, without spawning an entity or creating collectible drops. Its translucent material uses the same item geometry and UV silhouette with a derived white/purple 16×16 display palette. Its center stays fixed while a configurable 30-degree lean rotates around Y; the default revolution takes eight seconds with a small bob. Infusion emits 2–4 white/purple motes every three ticks, sampled across the upper chest and shoulders at 72–85% of player height. Each mote has its own size, 12–32-tick lifetime, acceleration and cubic curve: it lifts away from the chest, then approaches the bobbing core from a random angle. Birth positions remain fixed when the donor moves. There are at most 48 live motes per infuser; distance and the nearest-16-infuser budget still apply. These visual motes have no pickup entity or effect on resource accounting. A completed core shows restrained green particles.

The work rig and expression atlases are preserved. Positional wood impacts are triggered by authoritative work progress alongside swings; movement, waiting, recall and capacity pauses do not emit chopping impacts. Timing and renderer effects are separate from resource accounting.

## 0.2.2 visual refinements

The pedestal uses a quiet, symmetrical inset-square stone texture. Its preview center is 1.4 blocks above the origin, alpha 130/255. The wand's 16×16 sprite has a slim violet handle, silver collar and bright white/violet core tip. The Magica HUD is seven GUI pixels high, with a purple fill and white highlight below a raised XP bar; health/food reserve the same extra vertical space.

Continuous arm cycles run while the synchronized chopping target is active. Head/body face the target and the normal vanilla held-item orientation is retained, with a wrist-position correction for the 13-unit arm. Standing blocks use vanilla destroy-progress packets; fallen log models project the same destroy-stage textures.

Show Work Area draws flat-colored translucent green (logging) and blue (storage) air cells above terrain. Shared interior faces are omitted: surfaces are seamless and texture-free. No world blocks or collisions are created. The area is one block high per column and refreshes as terrain/upgrades change.

Magica pickups have two physical display sizes: large orbs keep their 0.3 render scale; small orbs use 0.15 with the same white/purple material. The HUD and pedestal GUI show exact thirds using ⅓/⅔. Timber changes its grain axis at the fall midpoint so it lands already oriented horizontally.
