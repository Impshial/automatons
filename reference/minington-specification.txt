2. GENERIC AUTOMATONS AND JOB-AGNOSTIC TOTEMS

Minington is a job on the existing Automaton, selected in its GUI. It uses supplied pickaxes and shovels. A job change costs no new body, core, or Magica.

Totems are job agnostic. Any supported mix of Automatons can be assigned to the same totem. A totem can serve Lumbertons and Miningtons simultaneously, with independent work queues and common storage/upgrades where applicable. Do not lock the totem to its first worker's job or show only the most recently assigned worker's settings.

Keep personal inventory, home, identity, ownership, and health independent of the totem's shared work. Job changes retain items; incompatible tools become inactive and remain removable. They are never deleted or automatically converted.

EACH TOTEM CAN HAVE AT MOST ONE MINE:
- All assigned Miningtons use that mine's entrance, ladder, underground chamber, and shared progress.
- Extra miners join the existing mine instead of choosing additional sites.
- A temporary shortage of miners, changing every miner to another job, packing workers, unloading chunks, or reloading the world must not erase the mine.
- Adding a miner later resumes the same mine.
- A worker reassigned to another totem joins that totem's mine; it does not bring the old mine with it.
- A blocked, complete, or damaged mine remains registered. Do not create a replacement entrance automatically.
- Each totem has its own mine record and cannot claim another totem's registered entrance or overlapping reserved infrastructure.

Tunnel and Quarry are modes of this shared mine. Implement both. Use one active mining mode per totem, with Tunnel as the initial default. Mode selection affects assigned Miningtons only. Preserve each mode's progress when switching.

3. AUTOMATIC SITE SELECTION AND MINE REGISTRATION

Once an authorized Minington is assigned to a totem, it can start the mine automatically when it has appropriate tools, storage, and construction supplies. Players choose the mode through the totem GUI; they do not have to lay out every excavation cell manually.

Find a suitable site within the mining work area:
- Reachable from the totem and service chests.
- Space for the complete entrance structure, central shaft, underground chamber, and an initial work area.
- Suitable terrain and support, with no protected chunks, occupied containers, machines, other registered mines, or unavoidable immediate hazards.
- A valid orientation for the ladder, chamber, and work face opposite the ladder.
- Enough room for a complete initial 32-block tunnel leg and its first three-block sideways connection if Tunnel mode is selected, or the 5 x 5 footprint if Quarry mode is selected.

Prefer the closest suitable site to the totem, with stable tie-breaking. Search incrementally and consider other orientations/sites after a genuine rejection. A single path failure or temporarily unloaded chunk must not permanently disqualify an otherwise usable site.

Reserve the chosen location in server-owned totem data BEFORE construction starts. This reservation prevents two miners from building two entrances. Once the arch and central hole exist, register the location as the totem's mine. The registered mine initially has an access-under-construction state; workers must not descend into an unfinished shaft.

Persist the reservation, builder claim, structure placement progress, and registered mine identity. If the first builder changes jobs, is packed, or becomes unavailable, another eligible miner continues that same construction.

Use one active builder for the narrow entrance/shaft during initial construction unless additional independent tasks are demonstrably safe. Extra miners wait in a neutral state. Waiting for a colleague is not an error.

6. TUNNEL MODE

Base tunnel dimensions are one block wide and two blocks high. Each long leg is 32 blocks. The passage is level with the chamber floor, with solid support below it.

The first leg begins at the wall opposite the ladder. The work pattern is:
1. Excavate a 1 x 2 passage outward for 32 blocks.
2. At its end, select a valid left or right direction randomly.
3. Extend a 1 x 2 connection three blocks sideways.
4. Turn back and excavate a parallel 32-block passage toward the chamber's starting plane.
5. Continue alternating outward and return legs, using three-block sideways connections, while another complete pass fits the allowed area.

Use the following implementation defaults to make the pattern deterministic and resumable:
- Pick the lateral expansion direction at the first turn from the directions that can legally fit. Persist it and keep subsequent lanes expanding toward that same side.
- If both sides fit, choose randomly once. If only one fits, choose that one. Do not reroll the direction after unloading, saving, or reloading.
- A three-block lateral displacement means centerlines are three blocks apart, leaving two solid block columns between parallel one-block-wide passages.
- Count the initial long leg as 32 excavation columns, including its first column immediately beyond the chamber.
- Define each subsequent leg's endpoints from that first leg's start and end planes, avoiding cumulative off-by-one drift. Turning corners are shared cells, not extra length.
- A return leg ends at the original starting plane. It need not cut through the chamber's side, ladder, or supporting floor. The completed connected passages already provide a route home.
- Do not add deeper tunnel levels automatically in this implementation.

The miner works forward from a currently reachable standing position. The solid front face is a target to remove, not a location the worker must already be able to stand inside. Clear a face in a safe order with adequate overhead clearance and revalidate falling material.

Finish the current accessible face before advancing. Preserve the tunnel floor. Do not chase ore veins outside the planned volume or widen the passage around an obstruction.

The planner may use an already-cleared passage only after checking support, access, and ownership. Air is not proof of a safe walking surface.

Do not invent alternate entrances when a required passage is blocked. Try safe independent work within the same mine, or pause that frontier with a specific reason. A reach/path failure receives bounded retries and alternative standing-position checks.

A single narrow frontier may support only one worker at a time. Other Miningtons can perform distinct ready construction or excavation tasks where safe; otherwise they wait without a beacon error. Shared access does not require every assigned miner to swing simultaneously.

7. QUARRY MODE

Start the quarry from the chamber edge opposite the ladder. Its entry connects at chamber floor level.

The excavation has a fixed 5 x 5 outer footprint:
- The central 3 x 3 is the main open shaft.
- The outer one-block ring contains a continuous full-block staircase winding around the edge.
- The completed accessible shaft remains 3 x 3 in the center because the perimeter is reserved for access.

Use real cobblestone or dirt blocks to construct the steps and required support. Retain suitable existing terrain where it already provides the correct structure; placed blocks still consume supplied materials. These are full-block steps, not crafted stair items.

Excavate from the top down, maintaining access as work progresses:
- Plan the staircase in three dimensions, including turns, support, and clearance above each tread.
- Each walking transition changes height by at most one block.
- Every traversed step has enough clearance for the Automaton's collision box, including at corners and below higher turns.
- Build and validate access to the next working elevation before exposing a floor the worker cannot leave.
- Keep the staircase connected to the chamber and original ladder.
- Reserve step/support cells against every worker's destructive tasks.
- Never mine out a worker's current standing block or another worker's only route out.

Continue downward as far as lawful, safe excavation allows, bounded by the dimension's build limits, unbreakable terrain, available access, protection, and hazards. Do not impose a separate arbitrary 16/32/64-layer depth limit. Plan in small increments instead of scanning down to bedrock every tick.

A quarry should excavate all scheduled removable cells while leaving required perimeter access. It must not hollow out a deep 5 x 5 void and somehow add stairs afterward.

Small gaps in required access can be supported with the permitted real materials when reachable and safe. Check actual support/placement validity and stay within the quarry's reserved geometry. Do not silently bridge a large cavern outside the footprint, plug fluids, or perform unrelated construction.

Leave bedrock and other unbreakable cells intact. Continue other safe reachable excavation at that level. If they prevent further descent, report the actual stopping depth and remaining exclusions instead of repeatedly attempting the same block or claiming unreachable space was mined.

At completion, leave the staircase, entrance, ladder, and chamber usable. Mine completion never triggers removal of access infrastructure.
