Implement the following complete specification for my Minecraft 1.20.1 mod, working title Automatons. Inspect and extend the existing project, preserve working features that match this specification, and deliver a functioning mod with a compiled JAR. This document is the current design. Implement the features, recipes, artwork, interactions, and reliability fixes together.

The player flow is: harvest eligible vegetation and collect white-and-purple Magica; craft an Infusing Pedestal and Empty Magica Cores; infuse each core with 50 Magica through a visible transfer from the player; use charged cores to craft the Automaton Pedestal, wand, totems, body components, and upgrades; assemble a generic Automaton item in the Automaton Pedestal; place it in the world; select its job and provide appropriate tools in its GUI; then assign it to a totem. Multiple automatons share a totem and multiple nearby storage chests. Its original Automaton Pedestal remains its home.

The initial job is Lumberton. A Lumberton must reliably chop a reachable, eligible tree when it has the necessary tools and storage. Diagnose and fix the observed behavior where it approaches ordinary trees and then ignores them or enters an error state too readily. Ordinary scanning, navigation retries, temporary competition for a chest, and another worker claiming a tree are recoverable conditions, not fatal errors.

2. DEFINITIONS AND INITIAL BALANCE

- Magica: the independent white-and-purple magical resource collected by players.
- Empty Magica Core: a chargeable vessel holding from 0 to 49 Magica while empty or partly infused.
- Magica Core: the completed crafting ingredient containing exactly 50 Magica.
- Automaton Core: the torso component, crafted using two completed Magica Cores. It is a different item from a Magica Core.
- Infusing Pedestal: the small, black-and-white, claw-topped block that fills Magica Cores without a GUI.
- Automaton Pedestal: the full-size assembly block and home anchor, with a six-part GUI and a permanently visible rotating miniature Automaton display.
- Automaton Totem: the shared work anchor, chest discovery center, job dispatcher, and holder of upgrades.
- Each automaton has exactly three tool slots and, initially, nine separate cargo slots. An equipped tool occupies one of the three slots.
- Multiple automatons can be assigned to one totem from the start. Do not introduce a one-worker limit, a capacity-unlock item, or a population upgrade. Bound processing costs with scheduling budgets rather than an artificial single-worker rule.
- Default ranges are inclusive horizontal squares centered on the totem. Storage Range is 4 blocks outward, a 9 x 9 footprint, with chest positions within 4 blocks vertically. Lumber Range is 16 blocks outward, a 33 x 33 footprint, with rooted trunk bases within 16 blocks vertically. The storage area can also contain harvestable trees.
- Range shape, numeric ranges, cargo size, speeds, recipe material counts, infusion speed/range, and upgrade strengths are centralized starting balance. Implement the concrete defaults in this prompt and document them for adjustment.
- A completed Magica Core costs 50 Magica. The Automaton Core embeds two of these, so an automaton contains 100 Magica through its torso ingredients. Assembly does not deduct a second payment from the player.
- There is no ongoing Magica fuel requirement for working, job changes, assignment, packing, or deployment.

Roll one uniformly random integer in the inclusive configured range for each successful eligible player block break. A zero result spawns no Magica. Use these initial point ranges:

| Eligible source | Minimum | Maximum |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Recognized natural or grown tree log | 3 | 7 |
| Fully mature crop | 2 | 4 |
| Naturally generated or grown pumpkin/melon | 2 | 4 |
| Ripe berry bush | 1 | 3 |
| Eligible natural flower | 1 | 2 |
| Naturally generated or grown sugar cane, cactus, or bamboo | 0 | 2 |
| Naturally generated or tree-grown leaves, manually broken | 0 | 1 |
| Natural grass or fern | 0 | 1 |
| Immature crop, unlisted source, or ineligible source | 0 | 0 |

- Keep ranges configurable with tags and state predicates, documented precedence, and validation that minimum and maximum are nonnegative integers with minimum <= maximum. One block matching multiple categories receives one roll, not one reward per tag.
- The roll is the total point reward, not the number of orbs. Fortune, the number of loot items, or several drop callbacks must not multiply it. Preserve ordinary block drops.
- The initial trigger is an actual successful player block break. Evaluate the source's pre-break maturity/provenance. Cancelled breaks and client-side events award nothing. Deduplicate multi-block plants representing one logical harvest so a tall flower does not pay twice.
- Breaking one supporting block does not award extra Magica for neighboring plant segments destroyed indirectly. A subsequent direct player break of another independently eligible block gets its own roll.
- Player-planted crops and sapling-grown trees qualify after growth, including growth caused by bone meal. Naturally grown and bone-meal-grown versions use identical yield rules. Do not mark the entire generated tree as player-placed construction merely because its sapling was planted.
- Distinguish growth from placing an already-harvestable block and breaking it again. Track direct placement of logs, leaves, flowers, pumpkins, and similar eligible blocks as needed to prevent simple place/break farming. New growth can qualify even when its originating plant was placed by a player.
- Recognize existing trees through the same reliable structural/species rules used by Lumbertons. Document limits around old untracked placements instead of claiming perfect historical knowledge.
- Ordinary building blocks, stone, ores, leaf decay, explosions, indirect environmental destruction, machines, and automaton harvesting do not generate Magica in this scope. Merely right-clicking a plant does not trigger the block-break reward.
- Test inclusive boundaries, no duplicate rewards, source precedence, zero outcomes, and point conservation. Average yields are five per log and three per mature crop; approximately ten qualifying logs or seventeen mature crops fill one 50-point core on average, not as a guarantee.

5. INFUSING PEDESTAL AND CORE LIFECYCLE

- Create an Infusing Pedestal roughly half the height of the Automaton Pedestal, initially about 0.5 block tall. It has a black-and-white base and a claw-like top, using four short claws surrounding the floating core. Use Minecraft geometry and 16 x 16 block-face pixel density.

- Right-click an empty pedestal while holding an Empty Magica Core to insert exactly one. Support partially charged vessels as well. The actual stored stack floats and slowly rotates above the claws.
- Insertion binds the infusion to the inserting player's UUID. While that player is alive, in the same dimension, within the default four-block range, and has Magica, transfer points from that player's balance into this core.
- Default transfer rate: five points per second, one point every four normal game ticks. A zero-charge core takes ten seconds at normal tick rate when the player remains available and supplied.
- Transfer is atomic on the server: deduct a point and add it to the stored charge once. Clamp to the core's remaining capacity. Never overdraw the player or overfill the core. Two pedestals drawing from the same balance must not spend the same points.
- Leaving range, changing dimension, logging out, dying, or running out of Magica pauses infusion. Returning under valid conditions resumes it. Do not drain nearby bystanders or silently choose a new donor.
- At exactly 50 points, replace the vessel with the completed Magica Core representation without losing its contents. Stop draining immediately, emit green completion particles, and play a brief completion chime once on the transition.
- The finished core retains white-and-purple magical coloration; green particles are its readiness signal. Keep a restrained green effect active while it awaits collection. Do not replay the completion chime every tick or every reload.
- Empty-hand right-click retrieves the stored core, including its exact partial charge if unfinished. Transfer it to the player's hand/inventory only if it fits; otherwise leave it on the pedestal. Initial permissions restrict retrieval of an occupied pedestal to its donor/owner or an operator.
- A retrieved partial core can be reinserted and continues from its saved charge. The new legitimate inserter becomes the donor for the remaining points. Do not refund charge to a player and also preserve it in the item.
- Completed cores are consumed as crafting ingredients. There is no additional resource withdrawal at the crafting table or Automaton Pedestal. Recipe matching accepts only fully infused Magica Cores, never partial vessels.
- Empty zero-charge cores and completed cores may stack normally. Partially charged cores are non-stackable and show a concise charge value such as 23/50 Magica.
- Breaking the pedestal drops its stored core exactly once with preserved charge plus the normal block drop. Unloading/reloading preserves core charge and donor identity without catch-up spending for unloaded time.
- Validate both-hand interactions, rapid repeat clicks, multiple players, and block removal during a transfer. Only one core can be held at a time. Do not create a menu as a shortcut for this interaction.

- Determine capacity for the actual item type, stack data, stack limits, existing partial stacks, and applicable insertion filters. An inventory with no empty slots may still accept matching items. A chest with empty slots may reject a particular output.

Do not require storage for an entire large tree before allowing any progress when safe incremental processing and unloading are possible.

- Determine effective cargo fullness through actual insertion capacity, including compatible partial stacks. Occupying every slot does not mean full if the next collected output still fits an existing stack. Conversely, an incompatible new output may not fit despite free space in unrelated stacks. Use available capacity fully, preserve uncollected timber/output safely, and unload when the next actual required collection cannot fit; do not use a low percentage threshold or speculative whole-tree loot size to trigger early returns.
- Treat insertion simulation as a hint, not a guarantee. Execute transfers on the server and remove only the amount actually accepted. Keep remainders in cargo. Another player or worker can change the chest between checks.

Avoid duplicate items, negative counts, lost remainders, and several workers treating the same simulated free space as permanently reserved. Any capacity reservation scheme must reconcile actual inventory changes and release stale reservations.

Once the unloading trip has begun, deposit all accepted job cargo across available chests before resuming, rather than leaving after freeing only one slot. If some contents cannot be deposited, retain them and re-evaluate actual carrying/storage capacity. Clear resolved blockers and resume automatically when safe. This is recoverable inventory management, not a permanent error.

This includes returns for no available trees, tool restocking, storage trouble, a sustained path/work blocker, or any other return-to-totem reason. Keep the three tool-slot contents for work; only unload job cargo. Perform restocking during the same visit when needed.
