2. Separate settled requirements from adjustable implementation defaults.

Settled requirements include the specified appearance, 1.5-block height, Minecraft pixel density, expressive face textures, three tool slots, job-appropriate tool selection, totem-based upgrades, home pedestal, chunk protection, and a help beacon for missing tools, no eligible wood, full storage, or full cargo.

The following choices make this first implementation concrete, but are provisional balance/design defaults. Keep them centralized and easy to change, and identify them in your completion notes:

- One assigned automaton per totem. A pedestal can own multiple automatons and their totems.
- Square horizontal storage and lumber ranges centered on the totem.
- Base Storage Range: 4 blocks outward, forming a 9 x 9 footprint including the center block. Chest height allowance: 4 blocks above or below the totem.
- Base Lumber Range: 16 blocks outward, forming a 33 x 33 footprint. Search trunk bases within 16 blocks above or below the totem, then validate the associated tree within bounded tree-detection limits.
- Nine cargo slots, separate from the three tool slots.
- Speed has not been approved yet. Use the separately identified provisional speed targets below only as configurable starting values, not established user decisions.
- Four universal upgrade slots on the totem, with incompatible and conflicting combinations rejected.
- One public protection map per dimension for this first version. Protected chunks apply to all this mod's automatons. Record who created each protection; allow its creator or a server operator to revoke it. Respect compatible external claims where available.
- A 64-block-tall visual protection curtain. This is only the display height, not the protected height.
- Provisional recipes and upgrade values described below. Do not represent them as permanently settled balance decisions.

3. Implement the Lumberton model and textures.

- Give the Lumberton wooden construction, iron reinforcement, broad shoulders, long blocky arms, and a small square wooden head. Its flat face has two separate amber eyes and a small mouth. Include a small amber chest marking.
- The complete standing mob must be approximately 1.5 blocks tall. Give it a matching collision box and eye height, with a width suitable for ordinary paths and doorways.
- Hold the actual equipped axe in its hand. Do not bake an axe into the body model or render a tool that the entity does not own.
- Include idle, walking, looking, chopping, and carrying/returning behavior with restrained Minecraft-style animations. Keep held-item positioning correct during movement and swings.

4. Enforce Minecraft pixel density, not an arbitrary atlas size.

- New block-face textures and inventory item sprites must use a 16 x 16 base pixel design.
- Preserve approximately one texture pixel per 1/16 of a block on model surfaces at the model's final rendered scale. A model 1.5 blocks tall spans 24 such units vertically.
- An entity texture atlas may be 64 x 64, 128 x 128, or another suitable atlas size to fit all model faces and expression frames. A larger atlas must provide more layout space, not finer artwork per block.
- Build and unwrap the entity at its intended final size. Do not shrink a full-sized golem while leaving its UV density too fine, and do not stretch a 16-pixel face across a larger surface merely to force a 16 x 16 atlas.
- Keep cuboid dimensions and UV boundaries aligned to the pixel grid wherever practical. Use proportional UV coverage on smaller block-model faces as well.
- Use crisp pixels, simple shading, a limited palette, and nearest-neighbor texture sampling where applicable. Avoid blurred resampling, painted microdetail, smooth gradients in the material textures, and photoreal surface maps.
- Author the actual game textures and geometry. Document model dimensions, atlas dimensions, and the pixel-density convention for verification and maintenance.

6. Implement the Automaton Pedestal as the home and creation block.

- The pedestal is required to create survival automatons. Crafting a Lumberton must occur through the pedestal GUI, not by crafting a spawn egg in a normal crafting table.
- The pedestal GUI accepts the recipe ingredients, clearly shows whether the recipe is complete, and offers a Create Lumberton action.

- Store a persistent home pedestal identity and dimension on each created automaton. Store its owner and register the worker with that pedestal.
- A newly created Lumberton waits near its home until assigned to a totem.

- Call All Home is a persistent recall: workers stop initiating work, preserve their totem assignments and carried items, travel home, and remain there.
- Resume Work releases that recall and allows assigned workers to continue if their other requirements are satisfied. It does not invent assignments for unassigned workers.
- A falling tree already committed to an animation must settle safely, but recall must prevent starting another chopping action. Save remaining timber for later processing.
- Keep recall authoritative across save/reload, including workers that were unloaded when the button was used.
- If the home pedestal is destroyed, keep the worker and inventory intact, show Home missing, and provide a deliberate owner-only rebind action through the worker GUI and wand. Do not silently adopt a newly placed pedestal at the same coordinates or lose the mob.

- Distinguish no trees, no reachable trees, protected trees, missing tools, no storage, full storage, recall, and temporarily unloaded dependencies.

8. Define storage discovery and the two ranges clearly.

- Measure both horizontal ranges from the totem, using the same inclusive square-boundary rule everywhere. The inner Storage Range is not an exclusion zone for trees.
- Show both range and resulting footprint in the GUI, such as 4 blocks outward / 9 x 9.
- A chest is eligible only if it lies within the storage footprint and height allowance and can actually be reached and used by the worker.
- Discover nearby chests automatically. Show them in the GUI and let the owner disable one so a nearby personal chest is not inadvertently used. Use a consistent deterministic ordering, preferring reachable nearer storage.
- Support vanilla single and double chests without counting a double chest twice. Use item-handler access where appropriate for supported modded chest compatibility, but do not treat every nearby machine inventory as a Lumberton chest.
- Deposit collected wood, sticks, saplings, and other legitimate tree drops into eligible storage. Transfer only the quantity the chest accepts, and preserve the remainder in cargo.
- A chest may also supply appropriate replacement tools. Expose per-chest toggles for output and tool supply.
- Chests and totems may be inside protected chunks. Ordinary storage operations remain allowed there.
- If one chest is destroyed and another eligible usable chest remains, use the remaining chest. If all eligible chests are gone, return home while retaining the totem assignment and show No storage.
- Full storage stops harvesting, sends the worker to its totem, and activates the help beacon. Full cargo independently causes the same return and signal; the worker then attempts to unload into eligible nearby storage and clears that condition when enough space exists to proceed. Unreachable storage pauses work with a clear status and bounded retries. Do not discard cargo, harvest indefinitely, or loop pathfinding without a delay.
- A temporarily unloaded chunk is not evidence that its chest, pedestal, or totem was destroyed. Preserve the relationship and wait for a reliable check.
- When storage returns, resume only if the worker is not recalled and the other requirements are met.

10. Protect and revoke entire chunks, with confirmation.

- Protect: right-click a block to select its chunk. Display Yes / No confirmation before changing protection.
- Revoke: right-click a block in a protected chunk. Display Yes / No confirmation before removing protection.
- Closing the dialog, pressing Escape, or selecting No changes nothing. Confirm the original selected chunk rather than whichever chunk the player later looks at.
- Already-protected and already-unprotected cases should show a brief message and remain unchanged.
- A protected chunk covers its complete 16 x 16 horizontal footprint and the dimension's entire legal vertical range. Use the dimension's actual minimum and maximum build limits, including negative heights; do not hardcode Overworld bounds.
- Protection prevents this mod's destructive job actions. It permits walking, returning home, storage access, restocking, and unloading.
- The Lumberton must never cut a protected tree block, process protected fallen timber, or fell a tree into or through protected space. Check protection at the action itself, not only when discovering or assigning the tree.
- If a required part of a tree is protected, skip that tree rather than partially destroying it.
- Centralize action permissions so tree cutting, felling, timber processing, movement, and inventory interaction are checked according to the action being performed.
- Protection changes invalidate affected queued tasks. If protection is added while a committed fall animation is running, pause that job safely without yielding further drops or damaging protected contents; retain its state for later resolution.
- This is not a general claim system that prevents the player or unrelated vanilla mechanics from changing blocks.

12. Make the totem maintain the tree queue.

- While loaded and active, the totem continuously refreshes its knowledge of eligible trees using bounded incremental scans, cached results, and sensible delays.
- Do not scan the entire search volume every tick, force-load distant chunks, or recursively traverse an unbounded forest.
- Use a server-wide workload budget as well as per-totem limits so many totems do not all perform expensive work in the same tick.

13. Recognize trees conservatively.

- The Lumberton harvests actual trees, including valid trees grown by players, and ignores ordinary wooden construction.
- Do not identify a tree merely because a block is a log, or because a connected flood fill eventually reaches leaves.
- Validate a plausible rooted trunk, species-appropriate trunk and branch structure, and supporting natural leaf canopy. Handle neighboring canopies without absorbing an entire forest or attached wooden house.
- Use appropriate vanilla tags and extensible species definitions, rather than fragile display-name checks.
- Respect bounds on traversal, height, branch spread, and total blocks per candidate. If classification is ambiguous or exceeds those limits, skip it and provide a meaningful reason.
- Track directly player-placed structural logs going forward as an additional safety signal, while allowing sapling-grown trees. Acknowledge that old untracked logs and arbitrary modded structures cannot be perfectly classified through geometry alone.
- Support the ordinary Overworld tree families present in Minecraft 1.20.1, including 2 x 2 trunks and branched forms. Add explicit handling or report a deliberate unsupported case for unusual shapes rather than silently harvesting them incorrectly.
- Do not treat nether fungi, giant mushrooms, or arbitrary modded vegetation as supported trees merely because their blocks share a loose tag.
- Require the entire destructive job to fit the permitted work area and protection policy. Trees straddling an unsafe boundary are skipped.

14. Include controlled tree felling and grounded processing.

16. Use the totem beacon for every specified work-stopping condition.

- Maintain independent alert reasons: No usable tools, No eligible wood, Storage full, and Cargo full. The GUI must show all current reasons, not just whichever was detected last.
- Any of these reasons stops new harvesting, sends the worker back to its assigned totem, and makes the totem emit a vertical beacon-like light into the sky. Activate the signal when the problem is established, even while the worker is still travelling back.
- No usable tools means all three tool slots lack a usable job-appropriate tool. Return to the totem's storage area to restock, or wait for the player to supply an axe. Do not continue chopping barehanded or invent a tool.
- No eligible wood means the validated job queue contains neither a reachable permitted tree nor unfinished eligible fallen timber. The totem keeps scanning while the worker waits.
- Storage full means the registered eligible output chests cannot accept the relevant output, not that one chest happens to be full while another can accept it.
- Cargo full means the worker has insufficient capacity for the next safe collection/processing step. Stop first, return to the totem, signal, and attempt unloading into available local chests. Ordinary fullness must be able to resolve automatically through unloading.
- Fullness and missing storage are distinct. If all chests have actually been destroyed, return to the home pedestal and show No storage; do not confuse destruction with temporarily full chests or unloaded data.
- This is a custom help signal. It does not require a vanilla beacon block, mineral pyramid, fuel, or potion effects. Use amber/gold as the provisional beam color for all four reasons.
- Render efficiently from the loaded totem toward the dimension's upper extent. Keep the alert active indoors as well; do not require an unobstructed sky check. Rendering limits do not require forced chunk loading.
- Resolve each reason independently. A tool alert clears when the worker actually acquires a usable axe. A cargo alert clears after adequate inventory capacity is restored. A storage alert clears after suitable reachable output space is available.
- As soon as scanning discovers valid available trees or logs from an unfinished tree job, clear No eligible wood. If no other blocking condition remains and the worker is not recalled, extinguish the beam and automatically return it to work. No manual Resume Work button is required for recovery from an ordinary alert.
- Logs here means legitimate harvestable tree wood or this mod's fallen timber. It does not grant permission to chop a wooden building merely because its logs are nearby.
- If a new tree is found while the worker still lacks tools or storage space, keep the other warning active. The beam is visible whenever any supported alert remains unresolved. No single recovered condition can erase unrelated blockers.
- Recheck supplies and storage on relevant changes and at bounded intervals. Validate the selected job again before resuming so a stale observation or another worker's reservation does not cause a false recovery.
- The player can supply tools through the worker GUI or an eligible supply chest, free chest space, add another eligible chest, remove cargo directly, or grow suitable trees in the working area.
- Call All Home overrides travel to the totem: a recalled worker goes to its pedestal and stays there. Resolving alerts can clear the beam, but never cancels recall. Outstanding alert reasons can remain signalled at the still-assigned totem.
- If the totem is destroyed, clear the invalid job assignment and its beam, and return to the home pedestal while retaining inventory.
- Reassignment or worker removal clears the old totem's stale alert. Unloading pauses processing and rendering without erasing the persisted condition.

18. Keep movement and work speed separate, with provisional starting values.

- Single-trunk felling preparation: approximately 4 seconds with an unenchanted iron axe. Scale the preparation work with the number of trunk-base logs cut for a larger tree.
- Fallen-log processing: approximately 2 seconds per log with an unenchanted iron axe, before upgrades. This is separate from the tree's tipping animation.
- Falling animation: approximately 1.5 seconds for the controlled visual sequence, with bounded adjustments for tree size if necessary. Work-speed upgrades must not make the physical presentation unreadably instantaneous.

- The Work Speed upgrade changes chopping speed. Keep walking speed independently configurable.
- Animate swings in sync with work progress and apply drops/durability once at completion, not once per rendered swing. Tick-based progression should not multiply yield during server lag or unloaded time.
