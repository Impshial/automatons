# Lumberton

Give a Lumberton an axe and assign it to a completed **Lumber Yard**. It finds eligible trees through the site's shared work board, cuts them, processes fallen wood and unloads to local storage.
Different workers reserve their work so they do not pay out the same tree twice.

Natural trees and trees grown from planted saplings are recognized. Buildings, placed wood and nearby trees remain protected by the tree checks.
A whole tree can be cut at its rooted trunk's first, second or third log level; a fourth-level or side-branch cut does not start whole-tree felling.
Players need a stone-tier or better axe for automatic felling. An upper cut still removes the lower stump, and its first log is paid only once.

Trees animate as they fall, then become ordinary sideways log blocks. You can stand on or chop them normally. A Lumberton remembers the logs it created and processes those blocks; logs already removed by a player are not paid out twice.

Before travelling, the Lumberton checks where the tree will fall. It can clear removable obstructions in a limited fall area, collecting their drops. Protected areas, worksite structures, storage, placed structural logs and unbreakable blocks remain excluded. A blocked tree is deferred while it tries other trees.

Lumbertons cannot break blocks that need a pickaxe. They request a **Minington on the same Network** to clear those obstacles. The miner needs a suitable pickaxe and room for the drops; it keeps its mine progress while helping. Requests appear in **Logistics**, including when no miner is available. Lumbertons use dirt or planks for temporary climbing supports.

Harvest output is delivered to local chests between trees after roughly two stacks or one minute carrying a smaller load, giving Runningtons cargo to collect. Tools, replanting saplings and climbing supplies retain their normal reserves.

A background check reconciles stale falling-tree animations every five seconds. Actual logs use Minecraft block rendering and collision.

Carry saplings to replant after cutting. A worker that runs out can fetch more from local storage. Runningtons do not withdraw saplings, so keep that local supply stocked.
Lumbertons can use carried support blocks to reach high wood and recover those supports afterward. Unreachable or unloaded work waits safely.
